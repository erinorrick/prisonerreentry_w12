Same Offense, Different Clock

Two people, one offense history, eighteen years apart.

Your Role

You are a reentry planner in Colorado. Two people with very similar offense histories are about to come home, and you are responsible for both plans. One is 17. One is 35. Your job is to decide what developmental research says should be different about their plans, and what should not.

Jordan and Dana are fictional composites. Every research finding you see comes from this week's readings, and each one names its source.

JORDAN · AGE 17

Leaving a Division of Youth Services facility

  • Committed at 15 for an aggravated robbery committed with two friends; prior juvenile property charges
  • About 18 months in residential placement
  • Starting juvenile parole (Colorado requires at least 6 months), returning to live with his mother
  • Left school in 10th grade; earned some credits while committed
  • Assessed substance use treatment need; no work history
DANA · AGE 35

Paroling from a Department of Corrections facility

  • Starting adult parole, returning to live with a sister
  • Finished high school; worked construction on and off
  • History of substance use; a 9-year-old daughter

Part 1: Hot or Cold?

Icenogle and colleagues distinguish two kinds of maturity. Cold situations call on reasoning and planning when there is time to think; teenagers reach adult levels of this cognitive capacity around age 16. Hot situations call on self-restraint under emotion, excitement, risk, or peer pressure; this psychosocial maturity keeps developing past 18 and into the 20s.

Part 5: Make the Case

Rehabilitative perspective: Jordan's capacity to change is greater than Dana's, most serious adolescent offenders reduce their offending as they mature, and incarceration can disrupt the schooling that supports desistance.

Public safety perspective: adolescence and early adulthood are peak years for offending, Jordan's offense involved violence and peers, and the people Jordan and Dana harmed have a stake in how much weight age should carry.

Write 150 to 250 words explaining how and why your two plans differ. Include:

  1. The one or two differences you consider most important, and the evidence behind them.
  2. One place where you treated Jordan and Dana the same, and why.
  3. Both perspectives above, named explicitly, including where your plan for Jordan trades one against the other.
